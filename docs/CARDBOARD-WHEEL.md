# Building the cardboard wheel

No electronics are needed. The phone is the sensor, the paddles and the pedals.

> **The single most important rule:** drive with the wheel **upright** (facing you like a
> real wheel, tilted back no more than about 30°). The phone's motion sensors use gravity
> to correct drift. That only works when the steering axis isn't pointing at the
> ceiling. A wheel held flat like a tray *will* slowly drift.

## 1. Materials

* Corrugated cardboard (a delivery box), at least 3 sheets about 35 × 25 cm
* Glue (PVA/white glue or hot glue), a ruler, a pencil, a sharp craft knife, a cutting mat
* 4 wide rubber bands **or** a strip of hook-and-loop (Velcro) **or** double-sided foam tape
* Anti-slip material: rubber shelf liner, a cut-up rubber glove, or silicone bands
* Optional: red electrical tape (centre mark), grip tape or cloth for the handles

## 2. Size and shape

An F1-style rectangular "butterfly" wheel works best because your thumbs can reach the
phone's screen edges:

```
        ┌───────────────────────────────────────────┐
       ╱│  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐  │╲
      │ │  │                                     │  │ │
 GRIP │ │  │        PHONE  (landscape, screen     │  │ │ GRIP
  ~6cm│ │  │        facing you, centred)          │  │ │~6cm
      │ │  │                                     │  │ │
       ╲│  └─────────────────────────────────────┘  │╱
        └───────────────────────────────────────────┘
        ◄──────────────── about 30 cm ──────────────►      height about 18 cm
```

* **Overall width:** 28-32 cm (a real F1 wheel is about 28 cm wide). Wider gives finer
  control. Narrower makes the screen easier to reach.
* **Height:** 16-20 cm.
* **Grips:** 5-7 cm wide on each side, rounded. Your hands hold these at "9 and 3".
* **Phone window:** the phone's length + 4 mm by its width + 4 mm (for a Nothing Phone (2),
  162 × 76 mm, cut about 166 × 80 mm; add your case thickness). Measure your own phone.

## 3. Construction

1. Draw the outline on cardboard, then cut **three identical layers**.
2. Turn the middle layer 90° so its corrugation runs crossways to the other two. Cross-grained
   layers make the wheel much stiffer and stop it flexing. Glue the three together, weigh them
   down with books and leave them 1-2 hours.
3. **Phone bed:** on the front, glue a 4th "frame" layer around the phone window, but don't
   cut the window through the back layers. This makes a shallow pocket, 5 mm deep, that the
   phone sits in.
4. Glue a piece of anti-slip material to the floor of the pocket.
5. Round the grips. Wrap them in tape or cloth for comfort and durability.
6. Put a strip of **red tape at the top centre** of the rim (12 o'clock). This is your visual
   centre reference, like the marker on a real wheel.

## 4. Mounting the phone

* **Orientation:** landscape, screen facing you, centred left-to-right in the wheel.
  It doesn't matter which side the camera is on.
* **Securing (pick one):**
  * Rubber bands across the four **corners** only (never across the screen's middle), or
  * Two Velcro strips on the **back of the phone case** and in the pocket, or
  * Double-sided foam tape on the **case** (never directly on the phone).
* **Leave free:** the whole screen, the power button and the charging port (for long sessions).
* **Check:** shake the wheel. The phone must not move at all relative to the cardboard.
  Any wobble shows up as steering noise.

If your phone sits at an angle on the wheel (crooked, or tilted inside a deep pocket), run
**Calibrate axis** in the app (see [CALIBRATION.md](CALIBRATION.md)). The app then measures
the real steering axis instead of assuming one.

## 5. Holding it

* Hands on the grips at 9 and 3, wheel upright in front of you, about 30-40 cm from your eyes.
  Resting your elbows on the desk or armrests makes centring more consistent.
* **Left thumb:** downshift paddle (upper left of the screen) and brake (lower left).
* **Right thumb:** upshift paddle (upper right) and throttle (lower right).
* The extra buttons (DRS, ERS, clutch, pause) are in the top centre and are easiest to reach
  with an index finger or a quick thumb stretch.

## 6. Keeping a consistent centre

* Use the red 12 o'clock tape: when it points straight up, the wheel is centred.
* If you notice the centre has shifted, **hold the CENTRE button** at the bottom of the
  driving screen for about half a second with the wheel straight.
* The app's small centre dead zone (2 % by default) hides tiny hand tremors.

## 7. No cardboard? Still works

Hold the phone itself in landscape, upright and facing you, like a small steering pad, and
rotate it. Calibration is the same. The cardboard just gives you leverage, finer control
and a comfortable grip.

## Optional upgrades (not required)

* **Desk axle for a fixed centre and self-centring:** push a wooden dowel or a sturdy pencil
  through the exact centre of a cardboard disc glued to the back of the wheel, into a
  cardboard tube clamped to the desk. Two rubber bands from the wheel's bottom corners to the
  tube give a gentle self-centring spring. This makes centring repeatable and less tiring.
  Keep the axle roughly horizontal (wheel upright).
* **Physical paddle levers:** cardboard levers hinged with tape behind the wheel, with a
  capacitive tip (conductive foam or aluminium foil wired to a fingertip-sized pad) pressing
  on the screen's paddle areas. This is fiddly and only worth it if you really want the
  feel of real paddles.
* **Phone cooling:** a USB desk fan pointed at the back of the wheel during long sessions.
